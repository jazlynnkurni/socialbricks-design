Fostr Chronicle · Case Ledger

Five ways to hand a caseworker their time back.

The Case Ledger opens each visit note already filled with what the agency knows, so the worker only captures what's new. Every field that came from the record is a field they didn't retype. These five prototypes each make that saved time visible — in a different voice.

“It should feel like you're someone working with all the tools already on your desk, so you can tinker away to your heart's content.”
— Abraham, on the vibe

The Work Queue
The answer to the design grill: a single ranked queue sorted by what's due — decision-based filters (not the alphabet), one primary action, provenance on every row, real density.
ranked queue · scalable

The Workbench, optimized

The 5 minutes-saved directions

01
The Ledger
Double-entry made literal. The old way is a debit of minutes; every auto-filled field is a credit. A running balance of time saved.
accounting · monospace
02
The Workbench
Abraham's metaphor, direct. Field-tiles laid out on a desk; you arrange what's new and a stopwatch tallies the time returned.
tactile · tools on the desk
03
Quiet Capture
The anti-form. One field at a time, mostly just confirming what's already known, ending on a calm “11 minutes back.”
minimal · one flow
04
The Instrument Panel
A cockpit for your work. Minutes-saved as the hero KPI, a sparkline of saved-over-time, the field-reuse breakdown, the pipeline.
data-dense · glanceable
05
The Command Deck
A keyboard-first power tool. ⌘K palette, dense caseload, and a live minutes-saved counter that ticks as you file.
keyboard-first · dense
